# v0.9 The CSP Program, Step 2: L-CA Driven by the Program

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** Complete (step 2, `kpu-run` split out as 2b); step 2b next
**Tests:** test_csp_driver 6/6; full suite 230/230

## 1. Summary

`CspDriver` makes the cycle-level executor interpret a CSP program, with no `ScheduleResult` and
no generator. Each program action goes to its executor process. On S1, matmul 256³/32³ computes
the L0 reference's values bit for bit, equal to L-B, and reads DRAM exactly as often as the
program loads (128).

## 2. Decisions

- **Residency rides on the descriptor, and a program load never hits.**
  - `TileDescriptor::l3_consumers` seeds the L3 entry with the residency's consumer count.
  - The tag-CAM hit path is closed to program loads: a tile still held by its previous
    residency is waited out.
  - So DRAM reads equal the program's Loads, reloads included (tested at L3 sizes 4, 8, 16).
- **The dma process takes its credits in program order.** A Load is handed over once the
  previous one holds its credit. Out of order, a later load could take the slot an earlier
  one's consumers needed (hold-and-wait).
- **The engine is the executor's choice.** At level 1 the engines are lanes of one dma process.
  A fixed round-robin binding starved stores behind loads on the whole-tile path (first store
  at cycle 23,262 against 5,779), because each engine posts one tile at a time.
- **Accumulating matmul calls** (`MatMulComputeSpec::accumulate`). L0 has one MatMulAccum per
  k-slice:
  - each call adds to the accumulator the previous call left in the fabric, and is ordered
    after it;
  - the result is published to DRAIN only after the tile's last scheduled call;
  - the chain fills on its first call and drains on its last.
  - The generator path (one compute over all slices) is unchanged.
- **`kpu-run` is step 2b.** `run_at` takes a device model, not the spec L-CA needs, and the
  corpus tests iterate every implemented level. Flipping the flag would have run LU and
  spec-less devices at L-CA.

## 3. Measured (S1, matmul 256³/32³)

| | CSP program | generator |
|---|---|---|
| cycles, whole-tile DMA | 43,968 | 36,668 |
| cycles, DMA window 32 | 38,089 | 35,813 |
| first compute | 1,465 | 10,077 |
| DRAM loads | 128 | 128 |
| compute busy | 6,144 | 6,144 |

- **Compute starts about 7 times sooner.** That is the "DMA traffic needed to get the compute
  going" the architect asked to emulate.
- **The run ends 6-20% later, with a longer store tail after the last compute.**
- **Not yet attributed.** Three experiments ruled causes out:
  - no load gating made it slower (49,264);
  - a fill per k-slice wasn't it (fixed anyway: the chain fills once);
  - round-robin engines were part of it (fixed).
- The step-5 record is the tool for the rest.

## 4. Wrong Decisions

- **Round-robin engine binding in the driver's first version.** It made stores wait behind
  loads until every load had finished. Binding is the interpreter's choice at level 1, and the
  executor's tile-affine choice is better.
- **A fill and drain charged on every k-slice of an accumulation** (the step-2 compute model,
  meeting per-slice calls). An output-stationary chain fills once. Fixed. It did not explain the
  gap, but it was wrong.
