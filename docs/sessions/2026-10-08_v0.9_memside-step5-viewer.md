# v0.9 Memory-Side Debugger Step 5: The Viewer Decision Log

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** Complete (step 5); step 4 (checker) and step 6 (characterization) remain
**Tests:** test_viewer.py 7/7; full suite in the PR

## 1. Summary

After merging #335, the architect asked for the debugger next, to see the memory side outside
the NoC. Step 5 was built before step 4. `tools/visualization/memflow/index.html` reads a
`.mflow` bundle and shows:
- the run's bandwidth against the DRAM ceiling;
- every DRAM command per bank;
- the channel buses;
- the DMA engines' windows and requests;
- the store buffers and the port stubs, all on one cursor;
- an address heat map and an inspector.

## 2. Decisions

- **The ceiling goes in the record, not the page.** The page could compute the ceiling only by
  re-deriving the spec's DRAM rate. Putting `ceiling_bytes_per_cycle` and `burst_bytes` in the
  manifest keeps one source, the harness, so the page and the CLI print the same number.
  Readers default both, so the format stays at version 1.
- **The page draws from the raw tables, not the LOD pyramid.** The T4 scenario has 6,400 bursts
  and 7,385 commands, which is small enough to draw directly. The pyramid stays in the bundle
  for larger runs; the page can switch to it when a run needs it.
- **The heat map's rows are the rows in use.** A run touches a few rows over a wide range, so a
  linear range left the map mostly empty (seen on the first render).
- **Bandwidth bins are at least 8 cycles.** Burst data windows are whole executor cycles. A
  64 B burst at 17 B/cycle per channel is recorded as 3 or 4 cycles, so 1-cycle bins showed
  spikes to 48 B/cycle against a 34.1 ceiling. At 8 cycles a bin can still read a few percent
  over; the plan notes this.
- **Observations, not invariants.** The page reports what the record contains (peaks against
  capacity, refusals, waits, conflicts). Pass and fail stay with step 4's `mflow_check.py`.

## 3. Verification

- The page was rendered headlessly (Chromium) on the T4 bundle. Whole-run and zoomed
  (2,000-2,400) views were checked by eye:
  - summary 90% of 34.1, outcomes 6117 / 170 / 113, matching the CLI;
  - REF windows across banks, and conflict marks under ACTs;
  - the store buffers at capacity while ejections wait.
- `test_viewer.py` checks:
  - the pack embeds every table, and a non-bundle is refused;
  - the version the page accepts matches the writer's;
  - the bundle carries the ceiling and all five station kinds;
  - under Node, the page's own `parse` reads every column it draws from the real bundle;
  - `stepSeries` and `valueAt` are exact on a hand-made case.
