# v0.9 L-CA Executes the Walker's L3 Placement (#399, L3 part)

**Date:** 2026-11-13
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** The L3 part is complete. The L2 staging part of #399 follows in its own PR; banks are #417.
**Tests:** gcc, the full suite: 313/313. clang, the full suite: 312/312. New: `test_csp_lca_place`, 3 test cases.

## 1. Summary

L-CA used to pick every L3 slot itself. A per-DMA-engine round-robin counter chose a Load's
slot, and `seed_l3` had a counter of its own. A Writeback's L3 slot was really its L2 slot's
number. The credit was a count, "some slot is free", taken from the machine's whole L3 (kpu_s1:
128 tiles), whatever the program's `l3`.

Now each action carries its residency's L3 slot, the walker's. The action that opens a residency
takes that slot's credit: it waits until the tile placed there before has left L3. L-CA's slots
hold the same tiles, in the same order, as L-B's and L-T1's.

## 3. Technical Decisions

- **Decision 1: split #399 into two PRs, L3 then L2** (the user's choice, 2026-11-13). L3 placement
  and L2 staging touch different processes. #399 closes with the L2 PR.
- **Decision 2: banks to their own issue** (the user's choice). #399's scope does not list them,
  and bank timing needs every program's `banks` updated to the deployments' 4. They are #417, in
  v0.10.0.
- **Decision 3: the per-slot credit lives in the L3 `TagCAM`.**
  - **Choice:** a claim per slot, taken with the credit and ended when the tile's entry leaves the
    CAM (`invalidate` to zero references).
  - **Alternative:** slot state in `CreditPool`, released at each `release` call site.
  - **Rationale:** an entry leaves the CAM on several paths: a Release, a written residency's
    ejection, a legacy Move. Ending the claim with the entry frees the slot on all of them, with
    no change at the call sites. The count pool is still taken alongside, so its accounting and
    the tests on it are unchanged.
- **Decision 4: a slot's residencies take it in program order (the turn).** My first version
  claimed a slot as soon as it was free. The test showed the right tiles per slot, but in the wrong
  order: a Load that runs ahead took a slot the program gives an earlier Writeback first. Each
  opening action now carries its turn, the number of residencies the program placed in the slot
  before it. A claim needs the slot free and its turn.
- **Decision 5: the timing test asserts what L-CA shows, not L-T1's result.** At L-T1 a program
  pinned to two slots is slower than the free one. At L-CA it is faster (2544 cycles against
  2783): its Loads arrive one pair at a time, each with the DMA's whole bandwidth. The test
  asserts that the pinned Loads stall on credit longer (1845 cycles against 1792) and that no slot
  holds two tiles at once, from the executor's events. It prints the makespans.

## 4. Issues Encountered

- **L-CA ignored the program's `l3`.** The credit pool was the machine's 128 tiles. A program
  written for 5 slots could keep up to 128 Loads in flight. Measured on the streamed program:
  3429 cycles pooled, 2783 placed. Fewer Loads in flight share the DMA's bandwidth, so the
  first ones land sooner.
- The event check first matched BlockMover names with the prefix `BM`; they are `L3[..]:BM`. A
  Writeback's `CREDIT_RELEASED` right after its `TILE_ARRIVED_L3` returns the L2 credit, not L3's.
  The check now skips it.
- To confirm the checks test something, I turned the driver's placement off once: the slot check
  failed 16 times, and the stall comparison failed.
- The generator's bias-row matmul needs one more L3 slot (the init's tile). The test takes
  `gen::slots_needed`.

## 5. Wrong Decisions

- **The first claim had no order.** It allowed any free slot to be claimed by whichever producer
  got there first, which reorders a slot's residencies against the program. The per-slot test
  caught it. **Lesson:** a credit per slot is not enough; a slot's users are ordered, as
  writes to one register are.
