# v0.9 Residency Across Operators: `inherit` and `retain` (kpu-run-csp-programs Step 4d.1)

**Date:** 2026-10-10
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** Complete (4d.1); 4d.2 (`.kpuld` carries `.csp`, `KpuDevice` runs CSP chains) next
**Tests:** test_csp_inherit_retain (new, 7 cases); 6 CTests (new: the two fixtures at every
level from the CLI, and each one's `.tflow` recorded and checked); full suite in the PR

## 1. Summary

Step 4d's language decision, implemented: residency across operators is written in the program.
- `inherit X;` opens a program with X resident: no Load, its slots held from the start.
- `retain X;` ends a residency with no Release: the slot and the tile outlive the program, for
  the operator after it. On a closed accumulator it drains and writes back into a slot it keeps,
  with its epilogue on the way, and never stores.

All three levels execute both. A two-operator chain gives the same values at every level as
the two oracles composed: `matmul_relu_retain` retains C = relu(A B) and never stores it;
`bias_inherit` inherits C, adds a bias and stores it.

## 2. Decisions

- **4d is split in two.** 4d.1 covers the language and the levels; 4d.2 covers `.kpuld` and
  the orchestrator. Each half can be checked on its own: 4d.1 against the oracle, and 4d.2
  against 4d.1's chains. Doing both in one PR would have tested the orchestrator on language
  semantics nobody had verified yet.
- **Two new actions, Inherit and Retain, not side information.** The stream carries them in
  program order, like Release, so every level and every consumer (record, stepper, timeline,
  printer) sees them where they happen. Passing an "inherited set" to `begin()` and a "retained
  set" to `finish()` would have made two more channels for what the program already says.
- **An inherited value is the operand's value.** At L-B and L-CA the tile enters L3 from the
  operand's values. A standalone run therefore has a well-defined inherited tile, and the L0
  oracle needs no change, since its ops read the operand. The orchestrator (4d.2) puts the
  retained values there.
- **A level's result is DRAM with the retained tiles laid over it.** A retained tile need not
  be stored, so DRAM may not have it, but the program computed it. Comparing every operand to
  the oracle then covers retained results too.
- **The validator charges a `retain` inside loops for every iteration at once.** The body is
  checked once, for a representative iteration. Charging the iterations' total when the
  `retain` is reached is exact at the end and conservative before, so an accepted program is
  safe. The price: a program that only fits because early iterations' retains come late could
  be refused. That is acceptable, and noted in the header. The distinct-tile rule (each
  enclosing loop variable indexes one dimension, alone) is what makes the total a product.
- **`inherit` comes first.** "Held from the start" is then literally true, and the validator
  counts the slots against capacity from the first statement, as decided.
- **An inherited tile may be stored without being written.** This is the store the chain owes
  a retained, unstored result.
- **The `.tflow` flags already had the meaning.** Seeded (1) and Held (2) were the L0 path's
  `initially_resident` and `retained_by_caller`. Inherited and retained slots set them, so TF9
  accepts an inherited slot nothing filled during the run, with no checker change.

## 3. Found while testing

- My first expectation of the retaining matmul's DRAM loads (8) was wrong: B's column panel
  is loaded once per (i, j), so the count is 12. All three levels agreed on 12, and the test
  was corrected.
