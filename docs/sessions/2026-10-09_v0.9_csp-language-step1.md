# v0.9 The CSP Language: ADR 0004, Plan, and Step 1 (the Language Core)

**Date:** 2026-10-09
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** Complete (step 1); step 2 (the linear operator: fusion, tile contexts) next
**Tests:** test_csp_language 5/5 (with 14 refusals); test_csp_driver 7/7; full suite in the PR

## 1. Summary

The architect identified that the program-level representation was missing: the form a human or
an AI assistant writes, and the CSP engine executes. ADR 0002 §6.1 had decided there would be
none ("never hand-authored"). ADR 0004 reverses that, and `docs/plans/csp-language.md` defines
the language.

Decisions taken with the request:
- structured;
- implicit processes at level 1, explicit placement at level 2;
- KPUASM kept as the engine level;
- matmul, LU, and a linear operator as drivers, the last for fusion with vector processors on
  block-move ingress and egress.

Answers to the plan's questions:
- `{ }` blocks;
- residency explicit only;
- `.csp` with a version line;
- relu, gelu, silu, plus atan as the SFU physical-mapping test case;
- `movers.vector`;
- a bias as a resident vector operand.

Step 1 builds the core: parser, validator and compiler, printer, and an emitter from derived L0.

## 2. Decisions

- **Explicit residency, enforced statically** (the architect's reason): residency must be a
  property of the program, not of an execution order. That is exactly the failure of writing
  high-performance data movement in the stored-program and SIMT abstractions. The validator
  refuses:
  - a call on a non-resident tile;
  - `resident` twice, and capacity overflow;
  - releasing a written, unstored tile;
  - a store of an unwritten tile;
  - leftovers at the end.
- **A call's operands are co-resident.** This is why `csp::lower`'s action-granular IR is not
  printed: it can release an operand between another operand's feed and load. Derived L0 enters
  the language through an emitter that decides at call granularity. The plan is corrected.
- **Accumulation is per call, not per program.** The IR's Call carries `accumulate`. The
  behavioral interpreter used to infer it from whether the program had any Drain, which a
  program mixing an accumulator with in-place calls would have broken.
- **The printer is flat.** Loops are not recovered. Correctness here is the round trip
  (compile, print, compile gives identical actions, and the printed form is a fixed point).
  Loop recovery is a convenience for later.

## 3. Verification

- Written matmul, A held whole: 128 loads. Written with A's row panel reloaded per column panel:
  576 loads (64 + 8 x 64). Both are bit-identical to the reference. The residency written is
  the residency executed.
- Written LU (right-looking, all 16 tiles resident): bit-identical.
- Emitted matmul at L3 sizes 128, 16 and 4, and LU at 16 and 4: they compile, stay within
  capacity, and are bit-identical.
- At L-CA on S1, a written matmul 128³/32³ is bit-identical, with 32 DRAM loads, exactly as
  declared.

## 4. Wrong Decisions

- **The plan said the action-level lowering would emit through the printer.** It cannot, because
  calls are atomic. Found while designing the printer; the plan now says so (a deviation note in
  its step 1).
- **Two line numbers in my own validator tests were wrong, and the parser reported a missing `;`
  on the following line.** The parser now reports it on the line the `;` should have ended.
