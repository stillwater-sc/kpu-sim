## 3. Technical Decisions
- **Decision 1: a Store is two pushes.**
  - **Choice:** the L3 tile ejects the slot's beats into the DMA engine's store buffer, and the engine writes
    them to DRAM. The engine never reads L3.
  - **Alternatives considered:** a DMA read of L3, the plan's literal wording.
  - **Rationale:** credits up and data down. There is no request-response, as in `timing/`, where a
    BlockMover ejects.
- **Decision 2: the program's order governs DRAM.**
  - **Choice:** a burst waits while an earlier command's burst to the same bytes is unsent, when either
    writes.
  - **Alternatives considered:** refusing programs whose stores and loads overlap.
  - **Rationale:** real programs may load what they stored.
- **Decision 3: a Store or a Release is bound to the Load before it.**
  - **Choice:** it waits, which the deadlock detector finds, rather than faulting.
  - **Rationale:** this keeps the wait-not-fault behaviour.
- **Decision 4: separate read and write windows, and a landing buffer.**
  - **Choice:** the DMA engine always takes a read's response off the response link, and its writes have a
    window of their own.
  - **Rationale:** over the NoC a shared window can close a cycle of waits: reads wait on L3, behind the very
    ejections that the Stores drain.
- **Decision 5: the NoC port is a queue on the ring.**
  - **Choice:** the port sits between the two hubs of its fold link, on links.
  - **Rationale:** components meet only through links. The bubble rule applies per queue.
- **Decision 6: Fill for the hypothesized C.**
  - **Choice:** the DMA engine pushes L-B's tile into its slot at the program's Writeback, under the slot's
    credit, with no DRAM reads.
  - **Alternatives considered:** preloading at the start, or staging in DRAM.
  - **Rationale:** preloading is impossible when slots are reused, and staging adds DRAM traffic.
- **Decision 7: the DRAM layout is the plan's.**
  - **Choice:** each operand in its own 64 KiB-aligned region, tiles packed at the element width and padded
    to 64-byte beats.
  - **Rationale:** the walker places no tile in DRAM.

## 4. Issues Encountered
- **A ring-full deadlock without the bubble could not be built.** On the T4, T16 and T64, dimension-ordered
  routes are at most two hops a loop. The test checks what the rule refuses instead.
- **No DMA ↔ L3 traffic passes through a port as ring traffic**, so the port's ring-through path is tested by
  hand.
- **`DmaEngine::Config::write_window` was first placed mid-struct.** That silently shifted the positional
  initializers in the test rigs. It was moved last.
- **MSVC C1128 on `kpu_run.cpp`**, fixed with `/bigobj`.
- **`cycle_dram_trace_validator` failed once in a parallel run** and could not be reproduced in 13 reruns.
- **CodeRabbit's findings: 3 on #555, 10 plus a nitpick on #556, 1 on #557, 1 on #558.** All were fixed, each
  with a regression test that fails without its fix. The fixable NoC findings were:
  - the fabric's lifetime: its checks and the topology its hubs and ports point into are now shared with the
    simulator;
  - attach validation;
  - reset;
  - the port's wait counts;
  - a duplicate read response.

## 5. Wrong Decisions
- **Mutation runs that all reported "caught" while the suite was already failing** for an unrelated reason
  (the first dimension-order check). The lesson: rerun the clean suite before trusting a mutation result.
- **The first NoC bubble test claimed that a ring fills without the rule.** It never did on these layouts,
  and the test was rewritten to measure what the rule refuses.
- **Mid-struct field insertion in a positionally-initialized Config** (above). The lesson: append fields.
