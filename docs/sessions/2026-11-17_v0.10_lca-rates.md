# v0.10 L-CA Takes the Deployment's Rates; Calibration Reported (#458, partial)

**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Version:** v0.10
**Status:** in review. #458 continues: the PE grid (with #401) and the L2 banks (with #388/#417).

## 1. Summary

L-CA ran every deployment at the executor's default mover and streamer rates. Now it takes the
deployment's. kpu-run prints L-T1's makespan against L-CA's, which is the timing reference. Two
characterizations measured at the old defaults changed at the real rates; they are pinned to
their rates and tracked in #462.

## 2. Technical Decisions

- **Decision 1: bytes per cycle at the reference clock.** The executor's movers compute bytes
  per cycle as GB/s over `clock_ghz` (1 GHz), and the deployment declares no clock. So the
  mapping is gbps = bytes per cycle x clock_ghz, in one place, `csp_config_from`.
- **Decision 2 (the user's, 2026-11-17): the DMA rate is named, not mapped.** With a hosted
  DRAM the transfers are timed from the `memory.dram` parameters and `dma.bytes_per_cycle` is
  ignored (CodeRabbit's review corrected my first wording, which said the engine rate reached L-CA
  through the DRAM). I first described
  the no-DRAM path wrongly, as timing transfers at the controller's channel rate. In fact its
  bandwidth field is for statistics: a transfer costs a fixed DRAM latency whatever its size.
  Every reference deployment hosts an LPDDR5 DRAM, which is rate-true, so the user chose to name
  the gap without one and leave the legacy model. The channel rate is still set, to engines x the
  engine rate, for the statistics.
- **Decision 3 (the user's, 2026-11-17): calibration is reported, not held.** Timing answers to
  L-CA (CLAUDE.md). kpu-run prints the L-T1/L-CA makespan ratio, and CTests record it on two
  corpus programs. A tolerance waits for #401, which changes L-CA's fabric timing.
- **Decision 4: pin, do not loosen.** Two tests asserted relations measured at the old rates.
  Loosening them would hide what changed, so each is pinned to the rates it was measured at,
  with a comment, and #462 tracks understanding them at the real rates:
  - "the NoC costs time, never saves it": at the deployment's rates, T4 with the NoC is 2%
    faster;
  - "from a window of 16, within 2% of the whole program": now 11%.

## 3. Issues Encountered

- My first description of the no-DRAM DMA path, in the question to the user, was wrong. A test
  caught it: halving the DMA rate left the no-DRAM makespan unchanged. I corrected the description
  and asked again.

## 4. Wrong Decisions

- **Describing the legacy controller's timing without reading it.** Recorded in §3. The
  user's first answer rested on the wrong description; the second, on the corrected one.

## 5. Verification

- `test_lca_rates`:
  - the mover and streamer rates map, and the DMA's channel rate is set without a DRAM, with the
    gap named;
  - with a hosted DRAM, the DRAM model governs, and that is named too;
  - L-CA's makespan rises when the mover or the streamer rate falls.
- CTests `kpu_run_calibration_matmul_48x48x48_t16` and `..._linear_64_t16_relu`: kpu-run prints
  the L-T1/L-CA ratio.
- The two pinned characterizations pass at their rates.
- Full suites, `release-werror`, gcc and clang.
