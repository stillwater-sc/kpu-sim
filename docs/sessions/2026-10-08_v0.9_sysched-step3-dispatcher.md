# v0.9 System-Schedule Debugger Step 3: The Schedule-Paced Dispatcher

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** Complete (step 3); step 4 (the `.sflow` record and `kpu-sysim`) next
**Tests:** test_schedule_dispatcher 6/6; test_csp_spec_oracle (values at P = 0, 1, 4); full

## 1. Summary

`ScheduleDispatcher` releases a schedule's operations against a cursor, the oldest compute not
yet complete, at most P computes ahead. It answers the question the memflow view raised: why do
loads take L3 credits long before their data arrives? Pacing does cut credit-idle time. It also
exposed that the matmul schedule's reuse, which the plan called accidental, is real and large,
and that pacing alone destroys it.

## 2. Decisions

- **Release in schedule order, with a progress rule.** The executor's per-instance accounting
  needs operations in schedule order. Strict in-order release can wedge when a schedule places
  a later compute's operation ahead of the cursor's own compute, so those are released anyway
  and counted as forced.
- **P is optional in `ScheduleExecutor`, and unset by default.** Every existing caller, and every
  pinned timing, is unchanged. Setting P, even to unlimited, goes through the dispatcher, which
  lets a test prove unlimited P is exactly the old behaviour.
- **Tags come from data, not position.** A load serves the first later compute that consumes
  its tile, so PREFETCH_NEXT and BLOCKED_AB schedules are tagged correctly. (PREFETCH_NEXT
  turned out to force nothing even at P = 0. The forced-release test builds its own early
  load.)

## 3. Finding

On S1, matmul 256³ with 32³ tiles has 1,024 LOAD operations of 128 distinct tiles:

| P | Cycles | DRAM loads | Mean credit idle (cycles) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 174,725 | 1,024 | 1,467 |
| 1 | 121,464 | 768 | 2,106 |
| 2 | 128,738 | 782 | 3,595 |
| 4 | 112,586 | 777 | 4,590 |
| 8 | 80,492 | 530 | 4,428 |
| 16 | 62,274 | 359 | 5,221 |
| ∞ | 36,668 | 128 | 6,214 |

- **Released all at once,** the whole problem fits the 128-slot L3, and every repeat load hits
  the tag CAM, so each tile loads once.
- **Paced,** a tile's credit returns once its released consumers finish, and a consumer beyond P
  reloads it. B tiles are reused 8 computes apart, so P must reach 8 or more before B reuse
  returns.

The idle credit time the memflow view showed is, here, the price of keeping everything resident.
The real fix is a schedule that loads each tile once and keeps it until its last consumer. That
is the reuse-aware schedule deferred to the next plan (Q4).

## 4. Wrong Decisions

- **I first wrote the forced-release test against PREFETCH_NEXT, assuming it prefetches past
  P = 0.** It doesn't: its early loads still serve the compute right after them. The test now
  constructs the early load. My first construction picked an A tile the first compute also
  uses, which made it eligible and forced nothing.
