# v0.9 System-Schedule Debugger: Plan, and Step 1 (Round-Robin Arbitration)

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Version:** v0.9
**Status:** Complete (step 1); step 2 (compute fabric, `kpu_s1`) next
**Tests:** full suite 228/228; lpddr5_memory_controller_test 18/18; LPDDR5 pattern traces 18/19
(the 19th fails identically on the committed January trace)

## 1. Summary

The memflow view showed the T4's DMA engines served one after another. The architect asked how
to see the operator's schedule, then for a plan:
- one large L3 and one large compute fabric;
- round-robin arbitration;
- decided: one `.sflow` bundle; P as a schedule parameter; `kpu_s1` = the T4 folded; reuse
  deferred; one burst per grant.

The plan is `docs/plans/system-schedule-debugger.md`. Step 1 makes the hosted memory controller
arbitrate between the engines that share it.

## 2. Decisions

- **Engines post, the controller grants.** The controller's free queue slots are its credits.
  Granting them from the controller's tick, at the top before it advances, takes tick order out
  of the decision. A burst posted in cycle t was first seen by the controller at t+1 anyway, so
  DRAM sees the same timing. Only the order changes.
- **The burst granted on a turn is the one on the least-loaded channel.** This was found, not
  planned. Strict post order made all eight engines walk their tiles in step, and the T4's map
  interleaves channels every 2 KB. So they all queued on one channel at a time: one channel busy
  89% of the time, 55% of the ceiling. A tile's bursts are independent, so the arbiter may grant
  them out of order. The rule stays one burst per engine per turn (Q5).
- **The legacy unhosted model stays first-come.** It has no DRAM, is off the system-schedule
  path, and carries pinned timings across many suites.
- **A tile path engine posts one tile at a time.** A second post is refused, and the engine
  releases its L3 credit as on a full queue before. Credits are not held for tiles stuck behind
  the arbiter.
- **The grant quantum is a model option, not the default.** With channel balancing, Q = 1 is as
  fast as any Q.

## 3. Bugs Found

- **The LPDDR5 tFAW window treated an ACT at controller cycle 0 as empty.** The arbiter now
  grants on the first tick, so the first ACT lands at cycle 0 and a fifth ACT followed 24 ticks
  later against a tFAW of 32. `mflow_check.py` M5 caught it.
  - Fixed: `kNever` marks empty entries.
  - The "tFAW constraint" test only counted reads. It now measures the ACT spacing, failing on
    the old code (21 < 24) and passing now.
  - The same pattern exists in the GDDR6/7, DDR5 and HBM2/3 controllers. Follow-up.
- **Observed, not changed:**
  - AUTOMATIC refresh refreshes a bank every `tREFIpb` instead of one bank per `tREFIpb`.
    Pacing it did not change the bandwidth gap, so it is left for the DRAM model.
  - INV-TIME-4 counts a 4-entry window against "more than 4", so it can never fire.

## 4. Verification

- `test_memory_side_harness`, new arbitration case: with eight engines, round-robin grants
  every engine within the first cycles, serves it to near the end, and never repeats one within
  8 consecutive grants. Fixed serves the engines in id order. A quantum of 4 gives runs of
  exactly 4.
- `mflow_check_selftest`:
  - M6 fails on an engine passed over, and is not applied to fixed priority;
  - a grant before its post fails M4;
  - versions 1 and 2, and an unknown arbitration, are refused.
- `kpu-memsim` on T4 `t4_mixed`: 91% of the ceiling with round-robin, against 90% fixed;
  `mflow_check` passes.
- `trace_validator.py` passes 18 of the 19 regenerated LPDDR5 pattern traces. The 19th
  (`random_trace`, INV-001) fails identically on the trace committed in January, so it was not
  caused here. The regenerated traces were restored, not committed.

## 5. Wrong Decisions

- **I first built round-robin in strict post order and measured 66% on `t4_mixed`.** Before
  finding the channel convoy I tried two things that were not the cause: a grant quantum, and
  pacing refresh (reverted). The data (per-channel occupancy) found it. Lesson: on a bandwidth
  regression, measure where the bus idles before tuning knobs.
