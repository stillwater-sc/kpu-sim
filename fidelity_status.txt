## Compare two fidelities:

  Key Comparison Points

  1. What Both Fidelities Share

  Both produce identical numerical results - the actual matrix computation is correct:
  test_single_tile_matmul_correctness: [PASS] All C elements equal K
  test_multi_tile_matmul_correctness:  [PASS] All tiles correct

  2. What BEHAVIORAL Provides

  - Functional correctness - validates your algorithm works
  - Operation counts - DMA loads/stores, BM moves, streamer feeds/drains
  - Instant execution - operations are memcpy (no simulated time)
  - No timing information

  3. What TRANSACTIONAL Adds

  From the test output above, transactional provides timing analysis:

  DMA: 520 cycles, BM: 400 cycles, STR: 360 cycles

  Plus with loops:

  5. How to Use the Trace

  The transactional executor exports a Chrome trace:
  # In code: txn_exec.export_chrome_trace("/tmp/trace.json")

  Open it in https://ui.perfetto.dev to visualize:
  - DMA, BlockMover, Streamer timelines
  - Parallelism and overlaps
  - Loop events (LOOP_BEGIN/LOOP_END markers)
  - Bottleneck identification

  6. Timing Configuration Impact

  The test shows how bus width affects timing:
  Default: 112 cycles  (64-byte bus)
  Fast:     96 cycles  (128-byte bus)

  And how loop latency affects overhead:
  Default loop overhead:  9 cycles
  Slow loop overhead:    45 cycles  (with higher latencies)

  Bottom line: Use behavioral for "does my kernel compute the right answer?" and transactional for "how long will it take and where are the bottlenecks?"
